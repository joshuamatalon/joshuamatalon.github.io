Research · public data · two sample deliverables

Sourced research. Every figure carries the file, the filter and the date it was read.

Two deliverables from public data. A two-page business brief on one county, computed by script from the Census Bureau's 1.1-million-row County Business Patterns file, with a numbered source on every number. And a lead database of every farmers' market in nine Western New York counties from the state's own directory, standardised, with every phone number and website checked live the day it was built. Neither contains a figure that cannot be reproduced from its source line.

The brief

  • The methods section says what the data cannot tell you: counts are establishments not firms, the Bureau adds noise to protect confidentiality, the self-employed are not in the file.
  • Built from the raw download by one script, so a brief for a different county, or all sixty-seven in the state, is a parameter change.
  • Typeset to a written standard: serif text at book measure, a sans-serif for tables and labels, three sizes, one accent.

The lead database

  • Nothing was corrected by hand. A dead site or an odd number stays as published and is flagged in words, with colour as support.
  • Duplicates by name and address are kept and flagged, not removed. The directory had none in these counties, and the sheet says so.
  • Contact names are the market managers the state publishes in its directory. No scraping of social platforms, which their terms forbid.
  • For any other state with an open directory the same script runs against it; for one without, the sourcing is done by hand and each row still gets its citation.

Download the brief, the lead workbook and the same list as CSV. Public data: U.S. Census Bureau, County Business Patterns 2023; New York State Department of Agriculture and Markets, Farmers' Markets in New York State.
