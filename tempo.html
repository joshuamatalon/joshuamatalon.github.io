Decision tool · Python + JavaScript · 2026 season

TEMPO. One number per player, and the reasons behind it, while the clock runs.

A fantasy-football auction moves about $2,400 across a table in three hours, and you get roughly ten seconds per player to decide whether the room's price is a good one. TEMPO is the tool I built to make that decision for my own leagues, then ran in four real drafts this season: two salary-cap auctions and two snake drafts.

What happened in the actual rooms

519 Vets auction
Yahoo · 12 teams · $200 · Sep 7
169 of 170 sales entered live, every price and buyer matching the official results; the last $1 pick of the night was added afterwards. Prices ran 3% over the model's predictions with a spread that matched the cross-room prior the risk engine was built on. Roster graded first of twelve at the room's actual prices, 42 points (2.2%) off the best roster money could have bought in hindsight. Two anchors landed at or under their numbers; the rest of the plan came in at $1 to $13.
The tool was rebuilt on the morning of the draft after 40 dry runs found it overpricing two anchors and scoring empty roster slots for free. Both are fixed; both are in the post-mortem.
Misfits superflex auction
Sleeper · 10 teams · Sep 6
No auction history existed for this room, so the market was synthetic: consensus value under superflex replacement, scaled to the room's money, with a Bayesian update as sales landed. 169 of 170 sales arrived from Sleeper's feed. Mean price error $3.10, bias under a dollar. The model overrated quarterbacks by about 12% and underrated receivers, which is recorded for next time.
Viva guillotine snake
Sleeper · 8 teams · 2 lives · Sep 5
A guillotine league eliminates the lowest weekly score, so the objective was rebuilt around floor instead of ceiling: half the 25th percentile, half the median, minus replacement, with a durability term from two seasons of games missed. That reordering moved McCaffrey from third to sixth on the board before pick one.
League 610 snake
Yahoo · 12 teams · Aug 26
The first live run, from pick one. Market-value grade A-. The retrospective found three real defects: a static opponent model that never updated on what the room was actually doing, a roster-need term too weak against raw value, and need-blind late picks. All three were fixed before the September drafts. The retro is honest because the next draft depended on it.

How it is built

  • Python engine: four projection sources blended, then a valuation over replacement per position, a Monte Carlo simulation of the other managers, and a re-optimised plan after every sale or pick.
  • The room model for the 519 auction is learned from that room's own 2025 auction, all 180 sales; replaying them reconciles every team to the dollar, and the $1 to $3 tail predicts within sixty cents.
  • Flask server with server-sent events; a single-file JavaScript front end; every sale journaled append-only so a restart rebuilds the state and any entry can be undone.
  • Every number on the card is produced by the same seven-step trace the Engine tab shows. There is no separate display logic to drift.
  • Verification before each draft: scripted dry runs through the live server and page, seeds recorded, invariants checked, then a hindsight grader that scores the final roster against the best roster the room's prices allowed.

TEMPO runs on the draft-night network and is not a public app. A live demo on a copy of a real room is available on request.
