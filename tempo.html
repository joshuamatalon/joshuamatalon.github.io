Decision tool · Python + JavaScript · 2026 season

TEMPO. One number per player, and the reasons behind it, while the clock runs.

A fantasy-football auction moves about $2,400 across a table in three hours, and you get roughly ten seconds per player to decide whether the room's price is a good one. TEMPO is the tool I built to make that decision for my own leagues, then ran in four real drafts this season: two salary-cap auctions and two snake drafts. The screens below are from a demonstration league with generic team names; the numbers in the results section are from the real rooms.

What happened in the actual rooms

12-team salary-cap auction
Yahoo · $200 · Sep 7
169 of 170 sales entered live, every price and buyer matching the official results; the last $1 pick of the night was added afterwards. Prices ran 3% over the model's predictions with a spread that matched the cross-room prior the risk engine was built on. Roster graded first of twelve at the room's actual prices, 42 points (2.2%) off the best roster money could have bought in hindsight.
The tool was rebuilt on the morning of the draft after 40 dry runs found it overpricing two anchors and scoring empty roster slots for free. Both are fixed; both are in the post-mortem.
10-team superflex auction
Sleeper · Sep 6
No auction history existed for this room, so the market was synthetic: consensus value under superflex replacement, scaled to the room's money, with a Bayesian update as sales landed. 169 of 170 sales arrived from the platform's feed. Mean price error $3.10, bias under a dollar. The model overrated quarterbacks by about 12% and underrated receivers, which is recorded for next time.
8-team guillotine snake
Sleeper · 2 lives · Sep 5
A guillotine league eliminates the lowest weekly score, so the objective was rebuilt around floor instead of ceiling: half the 25th percentile, half the median, minus replacement, with a durability term from two seasons of games missed. That reordering moved a consensus top-three back to sixth on the board before pick one.
12-team snake
Yahoo · Aug 26
The first live run, from pick one. Market-value grade A-. The retrospective found three real defects: a static opponent model that never updated on what the room was actually doing, a roster-need term too weak against raw value, and need-blind late picks. All three were fixed before the September drafts.

How it is built

  • Python engine: four projection sources blended, then a valuation over replacement per position, a Monte Carlo simulation of the other managers, and a re-optimised plan after every sale or pick.
  • For the auction it was built for, the room model is learned from that room's own prior-year auction, all 180 sales; replaying them reconciles every team to the dollar, and the $1 to $3 tail predicts within sixty cents.
  • Flask server with server-sent events; a single-file JavaScript front end; every sale journaled append-only so a restart rebuilds the state and any entry can be undone.
  • Every number on the card is produced by the same seven-step trace the Engine tab shows. There is no separate display logic to drift.
  • Verification before each draft: scripted dry runs through the live server and page, seeds recorded, invariants checked, then a hindsight grader that scores the final roster against the best roster the room's prices allowed.

TEMPO runs on the draft-night network and is not a public app. A live demo on a demonstration league is available on request.
