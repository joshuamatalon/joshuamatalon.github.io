Data extraction · PDF to Excel · public data sample

PDF to validated Excel. Every number checked against the source's own arithmetic.

The Federal Reserve's H.15 release prints a 43-row table of interest rates. Its PDF text layer has no table structure and runs words together, so a copy and paste gives one unusable line per row. This workbook rebuilds rows, columns and the three-level headings from the position of every word on the page, then checks itself: the Fed defines the published weekly figure as the mean of the five daily values, so each row's average is recomputed and compared. A row that does not tie is a mis-read cell, and it is listed first, not passed.

How the table was read

  • Every word on the page comes with coordinates. Words are grouped into lines by vertical position, numeric tokens are assigned to columns by order, and the heading level is inferred from the indent of the label.
  • Labels are rebuilt because the text layer drops spaces: "Treasuryconstantmaturities" becomes "Treasury constant maturities" through a fixed dictionary, and footnote digits glued to labels are stripped.
  • The Source tab records the document, its URL, the fetch date, the pages parsed, the method in one paragraph, and the known limits.

What a buyer gets

  • A workbook with About, Rates, Validation and Source tabs, formatted to a written standard and openable without any add-in.
  • A validation surface that a reader can audit in a minute: which rows tie, which do not, and by how much.
  • The same script runs against the next release, so a recurring extraction is a refresh, not a repeat.
