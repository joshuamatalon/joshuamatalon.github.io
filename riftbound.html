Reference site + phone trainer · August 2026

Riftbound is 23 verified decks from one collection, and a phone trainer audited against the rules.

The Battle Box holds 23 decks for a trading card game, built from one owned collection. Under them sits a card corpus I reconciled by hand against the printed cards and the full rulebook, and a script solves and verifies every list. Coach is a nine-step phone trainer whose audit re-derives every drill's answer from the rules text before the page can ship.

What is in it

  • The Battle Box has 23 deck cores and 18 legend modules, which makes 43 of the game's 46 legends pickable. Each deck has a tier, a pilot note, a matchup web and a pull sheet sorted by set and collector number.
  • One script checks every deck for legality, for ownership against a reserved competitive deck, against an exclusion list, and for interaction floors and rune-pool feasibility. Card names follow the printed card face, and an alias table maps pre-release names to printed ones.
  • Coach has eight phone-first views that you reach only from the path: the turn, paying, the fighting words, combat, the timing words, when to play, moving, and scoring. Goals are streaks, because sixty random answers produce a best streak of two against targets of four to six, so the coin-flip drills cannot be passed by luck.
  • The deck browser and Coach share one design system of two typefaces, one gold accent and hairline rules. Text contrast is measured against the AA threshold for small type.

How it was built

  • The corpus came first. I pulled 989 distinct cards from a community database that served printed text only, then reconciled all 63 official errata by hand, most of which the source did not carry. Equipment bonuses had to be read off card images because no database had them, and every name was matched to the printed card face. I read the full rules text and cited it by section for every design decision, and joined a collection ledger to the corpus on set and collector number.
  • The design started from the rules. I worked out what the scoring, damage and action economy actually reward, read the owned pool against that, and wrote a design constitution drawn from the multiplayer literature of older card games. It fixes tiers to observable properties, sets an interaction floor, accounts for threats and answers in every matchup, and enumerates what is excluded.
  • Construction is a global allocation problem. Demand per card across all 23 cores, every leader module and a reserved competitive deck must fit the owned counts, and the shared table resources must be assignable for every five-deck subset. The verifier checks colour identity, copy caps, leader and signature restrictions, two format ban lists, the exclusion list, the interaction floors and every leader-by-package variant. The docs, pull sheets and card-art browser are all generated from one JSON source, and the art cache is audited for the right game, card and printing.
  • Coach is generated and then audited. Keyword glosses come out of the corpus by script, rules are cited by section, and an audit reads the shipped page, re-derives every drill's premise and answer from the corpus and the rules, and fails the build on any disagreement. I calibrated the audit by corrupting builds on purpose. Simulated random guessing never passes, and informed play reaches 100%.
